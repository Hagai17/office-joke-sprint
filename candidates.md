# Candidates — One-Line Office Joke: 24-Hour Sprint

Fourteen office jokes were drafted and duelled pairwise on the judge's own
criteria: a real one-line joke that lands, a punchline that surprises and
pays off the setup in a way anyone who has sat in an office recognises, one
tight line, a fresh and specific detail of office life, and clear on first
read. Every candidate was verified by script to be within the 200-character
gate. (No fire-drill angle was used — a sibling entry for another office
bounty already took that one today.)

## Winner (159 characters)

I blocked 'focus time' on my calendar so nobody would book over it; within a week three people had, and one of them titled their meeting 'Focus Time (Urgent)'.

## Runner-up (160 characters)

I've signed the same coworker's birthday card eleven times; I know his name is Dave, he works on the fourth floor, and I would not recognise him in an elevator.

## Why the winner won

The runner-up is the freshest ritual in the field — the birthday card signed
year after year for a man the narrator could not pick out of a lift — and
its dry closing turn is clean. It loses the final on the judge's second
criterion, funny: the winner's punchline is a concrete object the reader
can picture in their own calendar, a meeting actually titled
'Focus Time (Urgent)' booked straight over the block that existed to stop
exactly that. The setup makes a promise (the block will protect the time),
the middle breaks it three times over, and the title in the punchline pays
it off with a specific, current office detail — focus-time blocks and the
people who book over them — rather than a generic complaint about meetings.
Both lines are a single tight sentence, clear on first read; the winner's
surprise lands harder, so it takes the duel.
